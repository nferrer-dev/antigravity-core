Ah, I know exactly what was causing that!

When the new HTML was injected into the chat container, the container naturally "grew" to accommodate the new text. I was calculating your exact distance from the bottom and forcefully snapping you back to that position, but I was doing it inside a requestAnimationFrame block.

Because of that split-second delay, the browser was rendering exactly one frame of the expanded container with your old scroll position (causing the text to jump down), before instantly snapping back to the correct scroll position on the next frame. This is what caused that hyper-fast flickering or "jitter".

I've fixed this by moving the scroll position calculation to be completely synchronous. It now calculates and forcefully applies the new scroll position before the browser is even allowed to paint the new frame to your screen, completely eliminating the one-frame jitter.

The Formation of the Solar System

About 4.6 billion years ago, a localized disturbance—perhaps a nearby supernova—caused a particular cloud of gas and dust to collapse in on itself. As it collapsed, it began to spin, flattening into a protoplanetary disk. The vast majority of the mass collected in the center, forming our Sun. The remaining material in the disk clumped together over millions of years, forming the planets, moons, asteroids, and comets we know today.

Earth formed around 4.5 billion years ago. In its early days, it was a molten hellscape, constantly bombarded by debris. A Mars-sized object is thought to have collided with the early Earth, ejecting a massive amount of material that eventually coalesced to form the Moon.

The Emergence of Life

Life on Earth began at least 3.5 billion years ago, likely around deep-sea hydrothermal vents. These simple, single-celled organisms thrived in the extreme conditions. Over billions of years, life evolved and diversified. The Great Oxidation Event, caused by cyanobacteria producing oxygen via photosynthesis, drastically changed the Earth's atmosphere, leading to the eventual rise of complex multicellular life.

The Cambrian Explosion, roughly 541 million years ago, saw a rapid diversification of life forms, leading to the ancestors of almost all modern animal phyla. From the oceans, life slowly conquered the land—plants, insects, amphibians, and eventually reptiles and mammals.

The dinosaurs ruled the Earth for over 160 million years until a catastrophic asteroid impact 66 million years ago wiped out 75% of all plant and animal species, including all non-avian dinosaurs. This mass extinction event cleared the way for mammals to rapidly diversify and dominate the terrestrial environment.
